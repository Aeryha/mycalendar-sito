MyCalendar

An Android calendar that adapts to you.

My Calendar: not one calendar built the same for everyone, but one you build around your own character and around the way you actually work, train, study or keep yourself organised. You are the one who shapes the answer to your own needs.

Private by design. MyCalendar has no server: there is no place for your data to reach us. Your events live in your own Google Calendar, and the app's configuration in the private folder of your own Google Drive, which no other application can read.

No advertising, no tracking. The app contains no advertising SDKs and no profiling tools. The detail, category by category, is in the privacy policy.

You don't lose your work. Because it lives in your Google account, the calendar you have built turns up again on any device, at any time, with a single sign-in.

Your calendar, built by you

  • Build templates for your own events and add them with one tap, instead of retyping the same title, duration and colour every time.
  • Attach whatever counters you like to each one, to keep track of what matters to you: hours worked or in overtime, kilometres covered, lessons taught, trips, shifts on duty, hours of study. This is not a list of categories we thought of for you — you define the counters yourself.
  • Add counters together as you please, even when they come from different event types: counter combinations give you a single total — for example every hour worked, whatever kind of shift produced it.
  • A statistics section shows how those totals move over time, with historical charts.

Quick entry, typed or spoken

Write or say what you have to do, in ordinary language, and the app turns it into events.

It works straight away, with nothing to download. The interpreting is done by a parser built into the app, which recognises dates, times, durations and your own event types — and which can draw several events at once out of a single sentence.

Artificial intelligence is not required. If you want it, you can switch on a language model that understands freer, less predictable phrasing; if you leave it off, or if it fails to answer for any reason, entry carries on working with the built-in parser. Pulling several events out of one sentence is a feature of the built-in parser.

Either way the interpreting happens on your phone: the sentence is not sent to any artificial intelligence service. For exactly what the microphone does when you dictate, see section 3.6 of the privacy policy.

For shift work: set the rotation once and be done

This is the feature built for anyone whose weeks run on a repeating cycle. Using combinations you describe your rotation as a sequence of days — say morning, morning, afternoon, night, off, off — where each step is one of your own event types with its own hours, and the days off are part of the pattern as empty steps.

Then you lay it down in one go: pick the day to start from, and the app spreads the sequence out one step per day. You can have it repeat a number of times you choose, run until a given date, or carry on indefinitely. Months of shifts in a single operation, rather than one event at a time.

Alongside those there are chains, which do something different and complementary: an event type can generate what follows from it — further events or reminders — with the timing you have set, either relative to the originating event or at a fixed day and hour.

Themes you can genuinely change

Colours, shapes, shadows, background images: the appearance changes in depth, not between three presets. The point is to make MyCalendar your app, including to look at.

And besides

Change your phone and find everything again

The work you put into building your calendar is not tied to the device you did it on. Your data does not live inside the app: it lives in your Google account — the events in Google Calendar, the configuration in a private folder of your own Google Drive.

So you can change phone, reinstall the app or start over at any time and on any device: sign in with your Google account and everything you had built comes back by itself. Your event types with their counters, your combinations, your themes, your language, your notes and lists, your scheduled messages, your connected accounts and your preferences. There is nothing to export beforehand and nothing to import afterwards: the saving happens on its own, while you use the app.

The one thing the app does not keep outside your phone is your sign-in credentials — they stay in the device's encrypted store and are never uploaded anywhere. After reinstalling you sign in to your accounts again: it takes a few seconds, and it is the price of not having your keys parked somewhere on the internet.

The folder on Drive is reserved for the app: it does not see, touch or ask for access to the rest of your files, and no other application can read it. The complete detail, category by category, is in the privacy policy.
